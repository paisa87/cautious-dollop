About This Site

I (Andrew Pais) am a professor in biotechnology who teaches introductory biology and advanced, lab-based courses while fostering course-based research projects and other creative pursuits with the assistance of AI. I started experimenting with AI in 2023 when my students began using it, and I have kept using it as an administrative assistant, educational tool, and soundboard for idea generation.

This site collects a select number of use cases, which I deem ethical and worth sharing. This includes prompts I've tweaked, the products/workflows I've built, and genuine surprises/misfires. This website is for my peers and educators, but it's also just a public record I can point people to.

I'll spare you the hype and doom. I'm just a science-enthusiast, creative, and parent trying to figure out how AI fits into the lab, the classroom, and everything else (And where I fit into life given the impacts of AI...)

Drafting Lab SOPs from Scratch

I pasted an outdated student lab handout into Microsoft Copilot and asked it to update the procedure for the new DNA Subway Blue Line. Instead of guessing, Copilot stopped and asked two clarifying questions — which saved a full revision cycle. The output was a Python-generated Word document I put directly into use in BTC181.

Producing Songs about Biology

Using music as a teaching tool, I write original songs exploring concepts in biology, including scientific understanding of evolution and possible mechanisms for life's origin. I record phone video takes and bring Claude in to build a clean composite, surgically remove hesitation artifacts, fix phrase timing, and master to streaming-ready loudness (−14 LUFS). An honest look at what AI audio production can and can’t do.

Honest Reflections

The good, and bad, and ugly. Take aways from my experiences so far.

✓

What's Working

  • First-draft generation for documents I'd otherwise procrastinate on (SOPs, grants, emails)
  • Troubleshooting in domains where I know enough to evaluate the answer
  • Explaining concepts in multiple ways for different student levels
  • Turning messy notes into structured, readable documents
  • Breaking writer's block — Sometimes you need a sound board to spark ideas
✗

AI Fails

  • AI messing with parts of orignal files I did not ask it to (e.g. quiz banks)
  • Drawing the wrong conclusions from literature and hallucinating references confidently
  • Losing my voice with AI. For both music and writing
  • Expecting consistent output across sessions — the same prompt can give different results
  • Confiding too much in AI (just ask it to roast you)
↻

Recommended Practices

  • Ask yourself if the request honors institution's policies and ethical codes (e.g. FERPA)
  • Ask for permission rather than forgiveness if you have doubts
  • Be transparent with your use of AI
  • Treat it more as a partner rather than a blackbox
  • Keep notes on what worked — this site is partly me doing that retroactively

For the curious: I'm happy to go deeper on any of these use cases, conversations, and outputs. Some of the summary is AI trying to capture my voice while I edit it behind it with my own thoughts. To get more directly from me, send me a DM using Linkedin or the form below.
