About This Site

I'm a professor in biotechnology who teaches general biology and technical, lab-based courses, fosters undergraduate research projects, and pursues creative pet projects outside of work with AI. I started experimenting with AI in 2023 when my students began using it, and I have kept using it as an administrative assistant, educational tool, and soundboard for idea generation.

This site collects a select number of use cases, which I deem ethical and worth sharing. This includes prompts I've tweaked, the products/workflows I've built, and genuine surprises/misfires. This website is for my peers and educators, but it's also just a public record I can point people to.

No hype. No affiliate links. Just a working scientist, creative, and parent trying to figure out how AI fits into the lab, the classroom, and everything else (And where I fit into life given the impacts of AI...)

Honest Reflections

For the peer educators panel — and anyone else who wants the unfiltered version.

✓

What's Working

  • First-draft generation for documents I'd otherwise procrastinate on (SOPs, grants, emails)
  • Troubleshooting in domains where I know enough to evaluate the answer
  • Explaining concepts in multiple ways for different student levels
  • Turning messy notes into structured, readable documents
  • Breaking writer's block — AI gives me something to react to, which is easier than starting from nothing
✗

What Didn't Work

  • Trusting AI-generated quiz distractors without fact-checking every single one
  • Asking it to compare papers when I hadn't read them myself first — it conflates details
  • Using it to generate literature citations (it hallucinates references confidently)
  • Expecting consistent output across sessions — the same prompt can give different results
  • Treating the first draft as finished — heavy editing is always required
↻

What I'd Do Differently

  • Build a prompt library earlier — I've rewritten the same prompts too many times
  • Verify AI outputs more systematically, not just when something feels off
  • Introduce AI tools to students explicitly, with guardrails, rather than ignoring it
  • Use it more for thinking out loud — it's a great thinking partner when I treat it like one
  • Keep notes on what worked — this site is partly me doing that retroactively

For the peer educators panel: I'm happy to go deeper on any of these use cases during the session. The conversation pages linked throughout this site show the actual prompts and responses — not cherry-picked successes, but representative examples including the ones that needed significant follow-up.
