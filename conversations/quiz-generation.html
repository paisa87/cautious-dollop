Generating Practice Quiz Questions

Gemini Teaching Date: [add date]
Context: I needed a set of practice multiple-choice questions for BTC181 students reviewing gel electrophoresis before a lab practical. I tried Gemini for this one. It was fast — but I had to fact-check every distractor carefully. This is one of the use cases I’d categorize as “saves time but requires expert review.”
You
Generate 5 multiple-choice questions for community college students on gel electrophoresis principles. Each question should have 4 answer choices (A–D). Include the correct answer and a brief explanation. Focus on: how DNA migrates, the role of the DNA ladder, interpreting band size, and common errors.
Gemini
Question 1 In gel electrophoresis, why does DNA migrate toward the positive electrode (anode)? A) DNA is positively charged due to its phosphate backbone B) DNA is negatively charged due to its phosphate backbone C) DNA is attracted to the agarose matrix D) DNA migrates toward the negative electrode, not the positive one Answer: B DNA has a negatively charged phosphate backbone, so it migrates toward the positive electrode in an electric field. --- Question 2 A DNA ladder contains fragments of known sizes. What is its primary purpose in gel electrophoresis? A) To confirm the gel has been run correctly B) To estimate the size of unknown DNA fragments by comparison C) To increase the migration speed of the samples D) To prevent DNA degradation during the run Answer: B The ladder provides reference bands at known sizes, allowing you to estimate the size of unknown fragments by comparing their migration distance. --- Question 3 Two DNA fragments of 500 bp and 1500 bp are run on the same gel. Which statement is correct? A) The 1500 bp fragment will migrate farther from the wells B) The 500 bp fragment will migrate farther from the wells C) Both fragments will migrate the same distance D) The larger fragment migrates faster in a low-percentage gel Answer: B Smaller DNA fragments migrate faster and travel farther from the well in the same time period. --- Question 4 A student runs a PCR product on a gel and sees a faint smear instead of a distinct band. What is the most likely explanation? A) The DNA ladder was loaded incorrectly B) Too little ethidium bromide was used C) Non-specific amplification or primer dimer formation occurred D) The gel percentage was too low for the expected fragment size Answer: C A smear often indicates non-specific amplification, primer dimer, or degraded DNA — all producing heterogeneous fragment sizes rather than a single discrete band. --- Question 5 What does it indicate when a student sees no bands at all in their sample lane but the DNA ladder bands are clearly visible? A) The gel ran too long B) The sample DNA is too large to migrate C) The PCR failed or the sample was not loaded D) The buffer concentration was too high Answer: C If the ladder is visible but the sample lane is empty, the most likely explanations are failed PCR amplification, failure to load the sample, or sample degradation.
You
Question 4, answer D — I want to push back. A low gel percentage would actually make it harder to resolve small fragments, not impossible. And the "correct" answer C is fine, but the explanation doesn’t mention degraded DNA as a distinct cause from primer dimer. Can you separate those?
Gemini
You’re right on both points. Revised distractor D: "The gel was run at too high a voltage, causing the DNA to overheat" (This is a more plausible incorrect answer — high voltage can cause smearing by generating heat, and it’s a common student mistake.) Revised explanation for C: A smear has several possible causes that students should be able to distinguish: - Primer dimer: Short non-specific products formed when primers anneal to each other; appear as a bright low-molecular-weight band or diffuse smear near the bottom of the gel. - Non-specific amplification: Multiple off-target products amplified due to low annealing stringency; appear as multiple bands or a ladder-like smear across a range of sizes. - Degraded DNA template: Template DNA that has been partially digested produces a smear of fragments of varying sizes throughout the lane. Each has a different cause and a different fix, which is worth discussing with students.

What I took away

  • Gemini generated five usable questions quickly — the structure and format were exactly right, and questions 1–3 and 5 were accurate without changes.
  • Question 4 had a weak distractor and an imprecise explanation that I caught because I know the content. Students wouldn’t have caught it, which is the problem.
  • The correction process was fast and the revised explanation was actually better than what I would have written myself on a first pass.
  • Rule I now follow: never use AI-generated quiz questions without reading every distractor as if I were a confused student. That’s where errors hide.
