Drafting Lab SOPs from Scratch

ChatGPT Teaching Date: [add date]
Context: I had rough procedural notes for a Chelex-based fungal DNA extraction (covered in BTC181) spread across three sources: open educational resources I'd bookmarked, student draft SOPs from a previous semester, and my own hand-drawn lab notebook entries. I used ChatGPT's camera feature to digitize the notebook pages, then fed all three sources into a single prompt and asked it to synthesize them into one clean SOP.
You
Here are my rough notes for a Chelex DNA extraction from fungal isolates. Please reformat these as a numbered SOP with a materials list, numbered procedural steps, and safety callouts where relevant. Keep the language direct — this is for students, not a publication. [Rough notes pasted in] - chelex 100, 5% solution in water - ~50mg tissue or a small scraping from colony - add ~200ul chelex to tube - vortex briefly - boil 10 min in heat block (100C) - vortex again - spin 3 min full speed - supernatant = your DNA, use 2ul for PCR - chelex is irritant, gloves required - label tubes before you start or you will regret it
ChatGPT
SOP: Fungal Isolate DNA Extraction with Chelex 100 Purpose: Extract genomic DNA from fungal colony material for use in downstream PCR applications. Materials - 5% Chelex 100 solution (prepared in nuclease-free water) - Fungal isolate material (~50 mg tissue or colony scraping) - 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes (labeled before use) - Micropipettes and aerosol-barrier tips - Vortex mixer - Heat block set to 100°C - Microcentrifuge capable of full speed (~13,000 × g) - Personal protective equipment: gloves, lab coat, eye protection ⚠ Safety Note: Chelex 100 is an irritant. Wear gloves throughout this procedure. Avoid inhalation of dry resin. Procedure 1. Label all microcentrifuge tubes before beginning. 2. Transfer approximately 50 mg of fungal tissue or a small colony scraping into a labeled 1.5 mL tube. 3. Add 200 µL of 5% Chelex 100 solution to the tube. 4. Vortex briefly (5–10 seconds) to mix. 5. Incubate at 100°C in a heat block for 10 minutes. 6. Remove from heat block and vortex again briefly. 7. Centrifuge at full speed for 3 minutes. 8. The supernatant contains your extracted DNA. Transfer to a fresh labeled tube or use directly. 9. Use 2 µL of supernatant per PCR reaction. 10. Store remaining DNA at −20°C or proceed to PCR. Notes - Do not disturb the Chelex pellet when pipetting supernatant. - DNA quality by this method is sufficient for PCR but not for applications requiring high-purity nucleic acid.

Common Problems No amplification in PCR after extraction - Likely cause: Chelex pellet was disturbed during pipetting, introducing inhibitors into the supernatant. - Solution: Re-spin the tube and carefully re-pipette from the top of the supernatant. If the problem persists, dilute the DNA 1:5 in nuclease-free water before using in PCR. Inconsistent results between replicate extractions - Likely cause: Variation in tissue input amount, incomplete homogenization, or inconsistent boiling temperature. - Solution: Standardize tissue input using a balance or consistent colony scraping technique. Verify heat block temperature with a calibrated thermometer. Ensure all samples are vortexed and boiled under identical conditions.

What I took away

  • The structure came out clean on the first try — Claude understood "SOP" as a format with materials, steps, and safety callouts without me having to define it.
  • The follow-up prompts (Common Problems, header block) were easy and additive — much faster than editing a blank template myself.
  • I still reviewed every step against my actual protocol before handing it to students. A few µL values and timing notes needed adjustment.
  • Total time from rough notes to final SOP: about 15 minutes, versus probably 45–60 minutes writing from scratch.
