Troubleshooting a Failed PCR

Claude Lab & Research Date: [add date]
Context: I ran a PCR that should have produced a clean band around 400 bp. The gel showed a bright smear with no distinct band. I had a full day of work scheduled and didn't want to spend hours working through a troubleshooting checklist from memory. I described the conditions to Claude and asked for a ranked differential diagnosis.
You
My PCR failed. The gel shows a bright smear and no distinct band. Here are my conditions: - Annealing temp: 58°C - Mg²⁺: 2.5 mM - Primers: designed for rbcL, Tm ~60°C each, ordered 6 months ago - Template: Chelex-extracted plant DNA, used 2 µL in 25 µL reaction - Polymerase: Promega GoTaq, fresh aliquot - Cycle: 95°C 2min, 35 cycles of [95°C 30s, 58°C 30s, 72°C 1min], 72°C 5min What are the most likely causes in order of probability?
Claude
A smear with no distinct band usually means product is forming but isn't resolving cleanly. Here are the most likely causes, roughly in order: 1. Primer dimer or non-specific amplification (most likely) At 2.5 mM Mg²⁺ and 58°C — which is 2°C below your primer Tm — conditions are permissive enough for non-specific binding, especially if primer stocks have degraded or concentration is off. A bright low-MW smear is a classic primer dimer signature. 2. Too much template / inhibitors in template Chelex extracts from plant tissue often carry polyphenols and polysaccharides that don't inhibit completely but cause smearing. 2 µL can also be too much depending on the extraction quality. 3. Primer degradation Six months is within range, but if primers were freeze-thawed repeatedly or stored improperly, 3′-end degradation can cause non-specific priming. How many freeze-thaw cycles have the working stocks seen? 4. Mg²⁺ too high 2.5 mM is on the high end. High Mg²⁺ decreases polymerase fidelity and promotes non-specific amplification. Standard for GoTaq is 1.5–2.0 mM. Suggested first step: Run a no-template control and a primer-only control (primers, water, no template) on a fresh gel. If you see product in the primer-only lane, you have primer dimer. That points directly to cause #1 and tells you to increase annealing temp to 62–64°C and/or drop Mg²⁺ to 1.5 mM.
You
The primer-only control had a faint band around 100 bp. So primer dimer is confirmed. What's the fix?
Claude
Confirmed primer dimer. Three levers to pull, in order of how disruptive they are: 1. Raise annealing temperature (least disruptive) Try 62°C first. Your primers have a Tm of ~60°C, so 58°C is too low — primers can snap back to each other at that temp. Bumping to 62°C should reduce dimer formation while still amplifying your target. If smearing persists, try 64°C. 2. Lower Mg²⁺ to 1.5 mM Drop from 2.5 mM to 1.5 mM. This increases stringency and is compatible with GoTaq. Run the Mg²⁺ change alongside the temperature change if you have enough reactions to test both conditions simultaneously. 3. Hot-start or touchdown PCR (if above don't resolve it) If you're still getting smear, a touchdown protocol starting at 68°C and stepping down to 58°C over 10 cycles can preferentially amplify specific product before primer dimer has a chance to accumulate. GoTaq doesn't have hot-start capability, but you can do a manual hot-start by adding the polymerase after the initial denaturation step. I'd run condition 1 and 2 together in a 2×2 matrix (58 vs 62°C × 1.5 vs 2.5 mM Mg²⁺) if you have the template to spare. That gives you four lanes of data and usually resolves it in one more gel run.
You
Raising the annealing temp to 62°C and dropping Mg to 1.5 mM fixed it. Single clean band.
Claude
Good outcome. Worth noting for your records: - Your working conditions: 62°C annealing, 1.5 mM Mg²⁺, 2 µL Chelex-extracted plant template in 25 µL GoTaq reaction. - The original 58°C / 2.5 mM Mg²⁺ combination was too permissive for these primers, likely compounded by primer working stocks being at or past their best. If you run this assay routinely, updating the SOP to reflect 62°C / 1.5 mM will save you this troubleshooting step in the future.

What I took away

  • Primer dimer was indeed the cause — Claude ranked it first and it was correct.
  • The diagnostic suggestion (run a primer-only control) was exactly the right move. I already knew this, but having it stated clearly confirmed I was on the right track.
  • The 2×2 matrix suggestion was useful framing — I ended up running just two conditions but the logic was sound.
  • This worked well because I knew enough to evaluate the answer. If I hadn't, I would have needed to verify the Mg²⁺ guidance against the GoTaq manual — which I did anyway.
