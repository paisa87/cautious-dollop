Writing Assessment Rubrics

ChatGPT Teaching Date: [add date]
Context: I needed a rubric for a lab report assignment in BTC181. I had a learning objective but no rubric template I liked. I tried ChatGPT first for this one. The first draft was generic — the real value came from the back-and-forth to make it specific to a community college biotechnology lab course.
You
Write a four-point rubric for a lab report submitted by community college biotechnology students. The learning objective is: "Students will accurately document and interpret results from a gel electrophoresis experiment." The four performance levels should be: Exemplary (4), Proficient (3), Developing (2), Beginning (1).
ChatGPT
Rubric: Gel Electrophoresis Lab Report Criterion: Documentation of Results 4 – Exemplary: Student accurately records all gel results with complete labels and professional presentation. 3 – Proficient: Student records most results accurately with minor labeling omissions. 2 – Developing: Student records some results but documentation is incomplete or contains errors. 1 – Beginning: Student documentation is minimal, largely inaccurate, or missing key elements. Criterion: Interpretation of Results 4 – Exemplary: Student provides a thorough and accurate interpretation of band patterns, including molecular weight estimation. 3 – Proficient: Student interprets most results correctly with minor inaccuracies. 2 – Developing: Student attempts interpretation but demonstrates limited understanding. 1 – Beginning: Student interpretation is absent or substantially incorrect. Criterion: Written Communication 4 – Exemplary: Writing is clear, concise, and uses appropriate scientific terminology throughout. 3 – Proficient: Writing is generally clear with occasional unclear phrasing or terminology errors. 2 – Developing: Writing is difficult to follow or uses inappropriate terminology frequently. 1 – Beginning: Writing is largely unclear and does not meet scientific writing expectations.
You
This is too generic. Make it specific to gel electrophoresis for DNA. Include the DNA ladder, band sizes, and interpretation of whether amplification worked or not. These students are running PCR products on gels, not proteins.
ChatGPT
Rubric: PCR Gel Electrophoresis Lab Report (DNA) Criterion 1: Gel Image Documentation 4 – Student includes a clearly labeled gel image with: lane numbers, DNA ladder lane identified, expected band size noted, sample identities in each lane, and a scale or size reference. 3 – Student labels the gel image but omits 1–2 required elements (e.g., missing ladder lane label or one sample identity). 2 – Student includes an image but labeling is incomplete — fewer than 3 required elements are correctly identified. 1 – No gel image included, or image is present but unlabeled. Criterion 2: Band Size Estimation 4 – Student accurately estimates band size(s) by comparison to DNA ladder, reports size in base pairs (bp), and notes whether size matches the expected PCR product. 3 – Student estimates band size but comparison to ladder is imprecise (±20%) or the expected size comparison is missing. 2 – Student attempts size estimation but method is unclear or result is substantially inaccurate. 1 – No band size estimation attempted, or estimation is not based on the ladder. Criterion 3: Interpretation of PCR Outcome 4 – Student correctly identifies whether PCR amplification was successful, provides evidence from the gel (presence/absence of band, size match, absence of smearing/primer dimer), and addresses negative and positive controls. 3 – Student correctly identifies the PCR outcome but does not reference controls or misses one piece of supporting evidence. 2 – Student states an outcome but interpretation is not supported by gel evidence, or controls are ignored. 1 – Student does not interpret the PCR outcome, or interpretation is incorrect without acknowledgment of error. Criterion 4: Scientific Writing 4 – Results and Discussion are written in past tense, third person, using correct terms (e.g., "band," "base pairs," "amplification," "electrophoresis") with no significant errors. 3 – Writing is mostly correct; 1–2 terminology errors or tense/person inconsistencies. 2 – Writing contains multiple scientific terminology errors or is written informally throughout. 1 – Writing does not meet scientific style expectations and impedes understanding of the results.
You
Much better. Add a criterion specifically for the Discussion section — students need to explain why their result makes sense (or doesn't) given the biology, not just describe the band.
ChatGPT
Criterion 5: Biological Interpretation in Discussion 4 – Student connects gel results to the biology of the experiment. For example: explains why a band of the expected size indicates successful PCR amplification of the target gene; or explains why a missing band might indicate failed extraction, inhibitors, or primer issues — not simply "PCR didn't work." 3 – Student offers a biological interpretation that is mostly correct but lacks specificity or depth (e.g., states the gene was amplified but does not explain what this means in context). 2 – Student describes the results rather than interpreting them biologically — essentially restates the Results section. 1 – No biological interpretation is provided in the Discussion.

What I took away

  • The first draft was exactly what I expected: generic and usable for any lab course, but not mine. This is the norm — the first draft is a starting point, not a product.
  • Once I gave it specific context (PCR products on DNA gels, ladder comparison, controls), the rubric became something I could actually hand to students.
  • The fifth criterion came from me knowing what my students consistently get wrong — AI doesn't know that. The expert knowledge still has to come from me.
  • I used the final rubric with one round of light editing. Probably saved 30–40 minutes compared to writing from scratch.
