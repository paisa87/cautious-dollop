Producing Songs about Biology

ChatGPT Claude Personal Teaching Spring 2026
Context: I write original songs exploring the scientific understanding of abiogenesis and themes of evolutionary history — biology as music, music as a teaching tool. The songs are meant to educate listeners about how life originated and evolved, set to original vocal performances I record on my phone. The song featured here, “It Was Good,” draws on the Genesis creation narrative as a lens for discussing the scientific evidence for abiogenesis and deep evolutionary time.

I record multiple phone video takes (.MOV files) and then use AI to do professional-grade audio production work: composite building from the best segments across takes, surgical hesitation removal, phrase timing correction, and multi-stage mastering. Earlier sessions used ChatGPT for initial splicing and vocal processing. This session documents the Claude Code work on “It Was Good” — six versions across an afternoon before arriving at a final master ready for web sharing.

How the composite was built: All three takes analyzed in 5-second blocks, scoring each on energy level and spectral clarity (mid/high frequency presence — a good proxy for vocal clarity). Take 74 dominated (16 of 22 blocks) — most consistent energy and presence throughout. Take 72 contributed 5 blocks (notably around 40s, 70–75s, and 80–90s). Take 73 contributed the opening block. 80 ms crossfades applied at every splice point; takes time-aligned to a common reference before selection.

Mastering chain applied: High-pass filter at 80 Hz → EQ cuts at 250 Hz (−2.5 dB) and 500 Hz (−1.5 dB) for mud → presence boost at 3 kHz (+2 dB) → air shelf at 8 kHz (+1.5 dB) → 3:1 compression (threshold −22 dB, 15 ms attack, 100 ms release, +3 dB makeup) → noise gate at −48 dBFS → normalized to −1 dBFS peak.

Claude
⚠ Phase cancellation artifact found at splice seam v4 The previous crossfades were placed at arbitrary sample positions. When two audio signals are summed mid-waveform at non-zero amplitude with even a slight phase mismatch, you get incoherent cancellation — rapid sign-flipping that sounds like a crackle or roughness. Rebuilt all three edits from the original take_74 with zero-crossing snapping (cut points nudged to nearest waveform zero-crossing) and equal-power fades (square-root curve). Seam artifacts eliminated.

✓ Natural decay preserved v5 “Life” peaks around 35.91–36.09s with a natural tail decaying through 36.32s. The previous splice cut at 36.38s, right as the word still had body to it. Moved the cut to 36.50s where the energy truly reaches silence, giving the word its full 120 ms of natural resonant decay.

✓ Natural pacing restored v6 In the original, “and it was” runs from 36.90s to 37.40s — a full 500 ms of natural phrasing before “good” hits at 37.74s. The timing edit from v3 had cut 270 ms right out of the middle of that phrase. Removed that edit entirely. This version has only one edit: the hesitation gap removal. The “good good good” section is now at its original, natural tempo.
Phase 4 — Final mastering to streaming loudness
You
Good enough, thank you. What else can you recommend to make this latest version slick and shiny enough for sharing on the web?
Claude
⚠ Loudness gap identified as the main issue The integrated LUFS is approximately −27 LUFS — about 13 dB quieter than Spotify/YouTube’s normalization target of −14 LUFS. The crest factor of 23.6 dB is very high for a finished track (commercial music typically lands 8–14 dB). Three things worth fixing:
  • Second-stage limiting to bring loudness to −14 LUFS (the biggest practical improvement)
  • Gentle high-shelf air boost at 12 kHz (+2 dB) for brightness and openness
  • Slightly tighter noise gate (−44 dBFS) to clean up between-phrase room noise

Phase 5 — Voice modulation (honest limit)
You
Can you make my voice in this song sound like a Johnny Cash-like singer?
Claude
✕ Honest limit — this is where the ceiling is Transparent assessment given upfront, no overclaiming Convincingly transforming vocal timbre to sound like a specific artist requires neural voice conversion — models like RVC (Retrieval-based Voice Conversion) or similar AI singing voice tools trained on actual vocal characteristics. That’s not something I can replicate with EQ and compression alone. I could make your voice darker and add some grit, but it would sound like a filtered version of you, not genuinely Cash-like.

Tools that actually do this well: RVC, So-VITS-SVC, or web services like Covers.ai or Musicfy. What I can do is prepare the cleanest possible dry vocal stem from your final master — isolated, noise-reduced, ready to feed into one of those tools.

What I took away

  • Claude Code had access to ffmpeg, numpy, and scipy, which made it genuinely capable of audio analysis — it could map RMS energy at 5 ms resolution to find exact hesitation boundaries, plot onset timing, and diagnose phase cancellation artifacts at the sample level. This isn’t "chat about audio," it’s actually running code on the file.
  • Six versions to get one phrase right (“life, it was good, good, good, good”) is not a failure — it’s an accurate reflection of how surgical audio editing works. Each iteration revealed something the last one introduced.
  • The loudness gap was the most practically important fix. At −27 LUFS, the track sounded thin next to anything commercially produced. Getting to −14.2 LUFS with a crest factor of 10.1 dB was the difference between a demo and something shareable.
  • The voice modulation limit was handled honestly and immediately — no overclaiming, no 20-exchange loop. That contrast with earlier ChatGPT experience (where the backing track loop went 20 rounds before admission) is notable. Getting pointed to the right tools (RVC, Musicfy) was more useful than a bad attempt.
  • Music as a teaching vehicle is underexplored in science education. If a song about abiogenesis can make a listener curious enough to look it up, it’s done more than a lecture slide.
